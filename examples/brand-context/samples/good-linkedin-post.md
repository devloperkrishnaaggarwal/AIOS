# Sample: LinkedIn Post — AIOS (Gold Standard)

## Post

Most business owners are still running their company like it's 2019.

Tons of SaaS tools. Endless SOPs. Automations duct-taped together.

You've optimized everything — and you're still the highest-paid administrator in your own company.

There's a shift I've been paying close attention to lately. Most people haven't caught on yet.

It's called an AI Operating System. AIOS.

Not a chatbot. Not another workflow. A methodology — a layer built around your business that actually knows your business and hands back the one thing you can never buy more of. Bandwidth.

The way I understand it, it comes together in 5 stages:

Context — The AI knows your team, your strategy, your offers. You stop explaining yourself from scratch every single time.

Data — Every number that matters, connected in one place. It knows where you stand, not just what you do.

Intelligence — Meetings, messages, org-wide signals — all logged. You wake up to a daily brief instead of spending your morning piecing together what happened yesterday.

Automate — You audit every task on your plate. Anything repeatable gets handed off. Your bandwidth starts coming back.

Build — That reclaimed time goes into what actually moves the business. The things that have been sitting on your "eventually" list for months.

I've been following Liam Ottley work on this closely.

Here's what I keep coming back to though:

Spreadsheets → SaaS → Automations → AI Operating System.

Every shift felt premature to most people — until suddenly it didn't.

The businesses building this layer now aren't just getting faster. They're building something competitors won't be able to catch up to easily.

The window is open. Not forever.

I'm building my own AIOS right now and documenting the whole process publicly — every layer, every win, everything.

If you're a founder curious about building one for your business—comment "AIOS" below, and I'll share exactly what I'm building and how.

## Voice Patterns Observed

- Opens with a bold, relatable problem statement — no intro, straight into the hook
- Short punchy lines mixed with longer explanatory prose
- Uses em dashes for rhythm and contrast
- No slang, no hype words
- Facts and logical progression build trust
- Ends with a clear CTA (comment + repost)
- First person throughout
- Uses "→" and formatting sparingly but effectively
- Builds curiosity progressively — reader doesn't know the full picture until the end
- References real people/work (Liam Ottley) to build credibility
- Creates urgency without hype ("The window is open. Not forever.")
